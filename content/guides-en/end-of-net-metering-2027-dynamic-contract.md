# End of net metering in 2027: what changes on a dynamic contract?

**Short answer:** from **1 January 2027** you can no longer offset the electricity you feed back against the electricity you take from the grid [1][2]. You then pay energy tax and VAT on **all** electricity you take from the grid, and you receive a separate feed-in fee for electricity you feed back [2]. Electricity you generate and use straight away stays free of tax [1][2]. On a dynamic contract, *when* you use electricity matters more.

## What is net metering?

Until 31 December 2026 your supplier nets, over the year, the electricity you feed back against the electricity you use, including energy tax and VAT [1][2]. If you feed back 2500 kWh in summer, you may use 2500 kWh in winter without paying electricity costs, network costs and energy tax on it, according to the Dutch government [1].

## What changes on 1 January 2027?

According to the ACM, from that date [2]:

- **No more netting.** Supply and feed-in are settled separately.
- **Tax on all supply.** You pay energy tax and VAT on all electricity you take from your supplier.
- **A feed-in fee.** Your supplier pays a *reasonable* fee for electricity you feed back. Until 1 January 2030 that is at least **50% of the bare supply price** (the price without energy tax and VAT) [1][2][3].
- **Feed-in charges can remain.** Suppliers may keep charging for feed-in, only for customers with solar panels. Those charges can sometimes be higher than the fee [2][4].

The law also says that, for a small connection, the fee averaged over a month **may not be negative** [3]. After feed-in charges are deducted, the ACM says a negative amount per kWh can still remain [4].

## And with a dynamic contract?

On a dynamic contract your feed-in fee depends on the current electricity price, which can differ by hour or by quarter-hour [2]. One example: Eneco writes that from 2027 dynamic customers receive the current hourly market price for all electricity they feed back, and that this rate can be positive or negative [5].

What that means in practice:

- **Feeding back in sunny hours often pays little.** The ACM points out that the market value of fed-back electricity stays limited [4]; on [Electricity price today](https://cerberostech.github.io/stroomprijs/en/electricity-price-today/) you can see how low the hourly price falls. Using it yourself saves the most: electricity from your panels that you use directly is not taxed and does not go through a supplier [1].
- **Feed-in charges at dynamic suppliers are low.** The ACM writes that customers of dynamic suppliers pay “little or no” feed-in charges, because those suppliers incur fewer costs for households with solar panels [6].
- **The risk is higher.** According to the ACM, dynamic contracts are only of interest if you can bear the risk of strongly varying rates [6].

Whether a dynamic contract works out lower for you depends on your usage, your generation and your supplier’s rates. We do not calculate that for you; compare the terms yourself. More on the differences is in [Dynamic vs fixed contract](https://cerberostech.github.io/stroomprijs/en/dynamic-vs-fixed-contract/).

## Getting more from your solar panels after 2027

The ACM advises using solar electricity as much as possible at the time you generate it [6]. In practice:

1. **See which hours have a low price.** On [Electricity price today](https://cerberostech.github.io/stroomprijs/en/electricity-price-today/) and [Electricity price tomorrow](https://cerberostech.github.io/stroomprijs/en/electricity-price-tomorrow/) you see the hourly market price. According to the ACM, the next day’s prices are published every day around 16:00 [7].
2. **Shift large appliances to sunny hours:** washing machine, dryer, dishwasher, boiler and charging an electric car. Tips are in [Cheapest hours for electricity](cheapest-electricity-hours-today).
3. **Measure what you feed back.** A P1 meter on your smart meter shows whether you are taking electricity or feeding it back.
4. **Add the low-price hours to your calendar** with our [calendar file](https://cerberostech.github.io/stroomprijs/goedkoopste-uren.ics).

## Does your contract run past 1 January 2027?

Check what your contract says about net metering. The legal scheme ends on 1 January 2027 and your supplier cannot postpone that date, but your contract may still give you a right to net. Your supplier must split your usage and feed-in into the period up to and including 31 December 2026 and the period from 1 January 2027 [2]. If you are unsure, ask your supplier.

Also check how feed-in charges are written in your contract. In the model contract that suppliers have had to offer since 1 January 2026, feed-in charges are calculated per kWh fed back [6].
